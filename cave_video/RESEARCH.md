# Boesmansgat, 2005: research notes for the Reel

Subject: the death of Dave Shaw on 8 January 2005 while trying to recover the body of Deon Dreyer from Boesmansgat (Bushman's Hole), South Africa, and the near-death of his support diver Don Shirley.

**Confidence labels**
- **WELL ESTABLISHED**: two or more independent reliable sources agree. These include contemporary news, the Outside long-read built on interviews with the team, and the peer-reviewed medical case report.
- **REPORTED**: one solid source, or sources that are not independent. Usable with light hedging.
- **DISPUTED**: sources conflict, or it is an interpretation. Hedge it or avoid it.

This is the medical analysis of the helmet video. Cronjé was DAN Southern Africa medical director and Meintjes was the expedition doctor.

## 3. Facts by topic

### 3.1 Boesmansgat
- **Location.** A sinkhole on the private Mount Carmel game farm near Daniëlskuil, Northern Cape, close to the Kalahari. **WELL ESTABLISHED.** [OUT] [IOL1] [WIKI] [ABC]
- **Shape.** A crater on the veld with a sheer cliff on one side and a small, swimming-pool-sized entry pool covered in duckweed. A narrow chimney below opens into an enormous main chamber below ~45 m (150 ft). A 1993 sonar scan measured the chamber at ~235 × 76 m (770 × 250 ft). The floor slopes. **REPORTED** (vivid and usable). [OUT]
- **Depth.** ~283 m. **WELL ESTABLISHED.** [WIKI] [GM]
- **Altitude.** ~1,500 m. At altitude, the lower air pressure at the surface means decompression must be planned as if the dive were deeper. Gomes's dive needed a schedule equivalent to ~339 m at sea level. **REPORTED** (the 339 m figure); the principle is standard dive physics. [WIKI citing Farr]
- **Water.** Clear ("gin-clear"; Shirley could see Shaw's light ~120 m below him) and ~19-20 °C. **REPORTED.** [OUT] [GUE]
- **Why notorious.** Deaths: Eben Leyden (1993), Deon Dreyer (1994), Dave Shaw (2005). Extreme depth combined with altitude. Very few people have dived below 250 m. **WELL ESTABLISHED.** [OUT] [WIKI] [ABC]

### 3.2 Deon Dreyer
- He was 20, from Vereeniging, and had about 200 dives. He was invited as a support diver for a South African Cave Diving Association expedition over Christmas 1994. **WELL ESTABLISHED.** [OUT] [ABC] [WIKI]
- On 17 Dec 1994, during a practice dive, he went missing on the ascent at ~50 m. Teammates saw his light sinking away below them. **WELL ESTABLISHED.** [OUT] [WIKI]
- The cause is unknown. The best guess is a blackout from CO2 build-up. Some accounts say oxygen toxicity or hypercapnia. **REPORTED / not determined.** [OUT] [WIKI]
- His body was not found. His parents, Theo and Marie, placed a plaque above the pool. Theo: "He had the most majestic grave in the country." **WELL ESTABLISHED.** [OUT] [ABC]

### 3.3 Dave Shaw
- Born 1954 in Katanning, Western Australia. He was a crop-duster, then a missionary pilot in Papua New Guinea and Tanzania, then a Cathay Pacific airline pilot from 1989, based in Hong Kong. He was married to Ann and had two children, Steven and Lisa. **WELL ESTABLISHED.** [OUT] [IOL1] [GM] [WIKI]
- He took up diving only in 1999 and progressed quickly to wreck, cave and rebreather diving. He was a leading advocate of closed-circuit rebreathers at extreme depth and used a modified US Navy Mk15.5 with oil-filled Hammerhead electronics. **WELL ESTABLISHED.** [OUT] [WIKI]
- Framing note: "relatively new" is accurate in years (about 5½ years), but by 2004 he was one of the world's most accomplished deep cave divers, with 300+ dives. Do not call him a novice or reckless amateur. **WELL ESTABLISHED.** [OUT] [WIKI/FINCH]
- On 28 Oct 2004 he reached ~270 m at Boesmansgat, setting several rebreather records, and found Dreyer's body by chance. **WELL ESTABLISHED.** [OUT] [GM] [IOL1]

### 3.4 The recovery plan (8 Jan 2005)
- Shaw would descend to the body and roll a body bag over the lower half. He would cut Dreyer free of his equipment, finish bagging him, and bring him up to ~220 m. There he would hand him to Shirley, and the body would be passed up a chain of divers and police divers. He was to spend only about 5-6 minutes at the body. **WELL ESTABLISHED.** [IOL1] [IOL2] [GM] [OUT]
- The total in-water plan was ~12 hours because of decompression. **WELL ESTABLISHED.** [OUT] [MG]
- Shirley had agreed to go down to Shaw "only if I signal", and said that if Shaw did not appear within ~6 minutes, "Dave becomes the priority." **REPORTED.** [OUT] [IOL1]
- Both men said that if they died, they should be left in the cave and nobody should risk a recovery. **WELL ESTABLISHED.** [IOL1] [GM] [OUT]
- The Dreyers waited at the water's edge with their minister. **WELL ESTABLISHED.** [IOL1] [GM] [OUT]
- Shaw wore a helmet camera (a Sony camcorder in a housing built by filmmaker Gordon Hiles). Because of the helmet, he could not sling his canister light around his neck as he usually did, and said he would set it aside when he needed both hands. **REPORTED.** [OUT]

### 3.5 What went wrong

**Established facts** (helmet video as described by those who viewed it, plus the medical paper):
1. The body was not a loose skeleton pinned to the floor, as expected. It came free of the silt and was close to neutrally buoyant, so it floated and rolled while Shaw tried to bag it. **WELL ESTABLISHED.** [OUT] [WIKI]
2. Shaw's breathing became harder and faster as he worked at ~270 m. **WELL ESTABLISHED.** [IOL2] [OUT] [MED]
3. He became entangled in the cave line he had tied to Dreyer in October. His light, which he had let go of, appears to have snagged the line. He held shears but did not cut free. **WELL ESTABLISHED** (entanglement); **REPORTED** (the light snag mechanism). [IOL2] [GM] [OUT] [WIKI]
4. The medical finding: the dense gas at that pressure greatly increased the work of breathing. Shaw could not breathe enough to clear CO2 while doing hard physical work. This set off a "spiralling crisis" of breathlessness and rising CO2, and extreme hypercapnia led to unconsciousness. He then drowned. **WELL ESTABLISHED** (peer-reviewed). [MED] [OUT: Cronjé]
5. The video records him losing consciousness about 21-22 minutes into the dive. **REPORTED.** [OUT]

**Interpretation** (label it as such if used):
- Narcosis probably narrowed his focus onto the task ("the body, the body, the body", said Peter Herbst). This is **interpretation.** [OUT]
- He might have survived had he abandoned the body when it floated free. This is the view of Gomes and Shirley, given in hindsight, and is **interpretation.** [OUT]
- Herbst's first-day remark that "he kept trying to cut the line" conflicts with the later frame-by-frame analysis, which found that he never cut anything. Do not say he "tried to cut himself free" as fact. **DISPUTED.** [IOL2] vs [OUT]

### 3.6 Don Shirley's emergency
- A British ex-Army electronics specialist, he ran a technical diving school at Komati Springs, Mpumalanga, and was Shaw's close friend and deep support diver. **WELL ESTABLISHED.** [OUT] [GM]
- He saw Shaw's light was not moving and went down after him. At ~244-250 m his rebreather's electronic controller was crushed by the pressure. He judged that continuing would mean dying beside his friend, and turned back. **WELL ESTABLISHED.** [OUT] [IOL1] [MG] [GM]
- During the long ascent and decompression he had inner-ear decompression sickness (a helium bubble in his inner ear), with violent vertigo and vomiting. Relays of support divers stayed with him. He was in the water ~12.5 hours. **WELL ESTABLISHED** (DCS); **REPORTED** (detail). [OUT] [GM] [MG]
- He was treated with 27 hours of recompression over two weeks. He was left with permanent damage to his balance, but has dived again. **WELL ESTABLISHED.** [OUT] [WIKI]

### 3.7 Aftermath
- On Wednesday 12 Jan 2005, while removing equipment, Herbst and Roux lifted the shot line with buoys. This pulled up the tangled cave line, and with it both bodies, which rose to the shallow part of the cave and were recovered. **WELL ESTABLISHED.** [IOL2] [GM] [OUT]
- IOL's headline line: "Dave Shaw did achieve his mission: he brought Deon Dreyer back to his parents." **WELL ESTABLISHED** (quote). [IOL2]
- The helmet camera was recovered intact. The police received a copy. Shirley and DAN Southern Africa's Dr Frans Cronjé analysed it for the investigation. **WELL ESTABLISHED.** [OUT] [IOL2] [MED]
- Shirley watched the video while breathing in time with Shaw, monitored for CO2, and nearly blacked out. "I actually died with Dave." **REPORTED.** [OUT]
- Lessons drawn:
  - The 2007 paper warned that gas density and work of breathing set physiological limits at extreme depth, "a timely and salient lesson to a growing population of deep technical divers". [MED]
  - Later research by Anthony and Mitchell, widely adopted by agencies and DAN, recommends keeping breathing gas density at or below about 5.2 g/L (6.2 g/L maximum). Exertion at high gas density risks dangerous CO2 retention. [DAN]
  - Expedition practice lessons, from Shirley and Herbst: never leave equipment loose, and when the plan breaks down at depth, abort. [OUT]
- **WELL ESTABLISHED** (the medical lesson); **REPORTED** (that the guidelines link directly to Shaw).

### 3.8 Shaw's motivation
- He offered without being asked and promised Theo Dreyer, "I will go and fetch your son." **WELL ESTABLISHED.** [IOL1] [IOL2] [OUT]
- Shirley: "Dave felt very connected with Deon… it was like a personal thing that he should bring him back." **WELL ESTABLISHED** (quote). [OUT] [ABC]
- Balance this with the adventure. Shaw joked to Herbst, "we're doing this for the adventure of it", and Hughes said, "it was another cave adventure." An honest telling says both. **WELL ESTABLISHED.** [OUT] [IOL1]

## 4. Myths and mistakes in popular retellings (avoid these)

| Claim seen online | Reality | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "Shaw ran out of air" | He was on a closed-circuit rebreather with gas available. He was overcome by CO2 build-up (respiratory failure from dense gas plus exertion) | MED, OUT |
| "The corpse grabbed him / pulled him down" | Sensational. Both were joined by a tangled cave line; the body was floating, not pulling him down | OUT, IOL2 |
| "He tried to cut himself free but the knife failed" | The first-day report said he tried to cut the line. The frame-by-frame analysis says he held the shears but never cut | IOL2 vs OUT |
| "His body was never recovered / both are still down there" | Both bodies came up on 12 Jan 2005, four days later | IOL2, GM, OUT |
| "Shirley reached him / dived to 270 m" | Shirley turned back at ~250 m and never reached Shaw | OUT, IOL1 |
| "Boesmansgat is the deepest cave in the world" | It was called the third-deepest freshwater cave (2005 sources) | IOL1, GM, OUT |
| "Shaw was an inexperienced novice / reckless" | He had ~5 years in the sport but was a record-holding elite rebreather diver with 300+ dives and a large, careful support plan | OUT, WIKI |
| "The Dreyers asked him to do it" | He volunteered | IOL1 |
| Exact depth "precisely 270 m" stated as fact | Sources vary from 263 to 272 m. Say "about 270 metres" | MED, GM, OUT |
| Dreyer's suit "drysuit" vs "wetsuit"; "perfectly preserved" | Sources conflict. Leave it out | OUT vs WIKI |
| "The whole dive was filmed and you can watch his death" | The footage exists and parts appear in documentaries. Do not use it, imitate it, or encourage people to seek it out | — |

## 5. Sensitive details to leave out
- **Leave out** the condition of Dreyer's remains: skeletonisation, the head detaching, the smell, a foot detaching, the morgue scene.
- **Leave out** the condition of Shaw's body on recovery (swelling, rigor) and cutting him out of his equipment.
- **Leave out** audio, re-creation or imitation of Shaw's final breathing ("gasping", "grunts"), and the "snuff tape" phrase.
- **Leave out** video stills from the helmet camera, and any depiction of the moment of death.
- **Leave out** Shirley's vomiting in detail. "Violent vertigo" is enough.
- **Leave out** private family moments: the Dreyers embracing the body, and Ann Shaw's private thoughts. Brief public quotes from the time are acceptable.
- **Leave out** blame toward individuals, such as the slate misreading or the helmet-camera decision. Present mechanisms neutrally.
- **Keep** Shaw's own wishes, and the Dreyers' plaque and "majestic grave" quote. These are respectful and public.
