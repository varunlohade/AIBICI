# 270 Metres Down: the Boesmansgat dive

_An animated true-incident Reel for Instagram (9:16). Narration + visual direction._

> Real people: told factually and respectfully. No footage of the death, no graphic detail, no invented drama. Every line is sourced in RESEARCH.md.

## Scene 1: Hook

**Visual:** Full cross-section of the sinkhole, surface to black floor. A tiny torch light starts sinking. Depth gauge ticks. Date slip: 8 JAN 2005.

**NARRATOR:** In January 2005, a diver went two hundred and seventy metres down a flooded sinkhole...  
**NARRATOR:** to bring a stranger home.  

## Scene 2: Boesmansgat

**Visual:** Surface: a small duckweed pool on a dry farm. Push down through the narrow throat into a vast flooded chamber. 1994: a second, older light sinks away and goes still on the floor (a soft glow, never a body).

**NARRATOR:** Boesmansgat, South Africa: a small pool above a flooded chamber nearly three hundred metres deep.  
**NARRATOR:** In 1994, a twenty-year-old diver named Deon Dreyer sank out of sight in it, and never came back up.  
**NARRATOR:** His body stayed down there for ten years.  

## Scene 3: Dave Shaw

**Visual:** Silhouette of a diver with a rebreather; a pilot's wings pin. 2004: his torch cone sweeps the floor and finds the soft glow. He ties his line to it. Then: an offer, two silhouettes at the water's edge.

**NARRATOR:** In 2004, Dave Shaw, an Australian pilot and record-breaking diver, reached the bottom.  
**NARRATOR:** His torch found Deon.  
**NARRATOR:** Nobody asked him to go back. He offered.  

## Scene 4: The Plan

**Visual:** A dive plan drawn on the cross-section: a fast line down, a 6-minute box at the bottom, then a long staircase of decompression stops up the side, labelled 12 hours. Support divers' lights at stations.

**NARRATOR:** The plan: a few minutes at the bottom, then twelve hours climbing slowly back to the light.  

## Scene 5: What Went Wrong

**Visual:** Bottom, -270 m. Gas molecules in the breathing loop drawn packed tight (density). The line, a thin bright thread, loops around the torch light. CO2 icon fills; red warning pulses; the torch stops moving.

**NARRATOR:** At two hundred and seventy metres, the gas you breathe is so dense that every breath is hard work.  
**NARRATOR:** As Dave worked, the body came loose, and his line tangled around him.  
**NARRATOR:** Straining against it, he couldn't clear the carbon dioxide from his lungs.  
**NARRATOR:** It built up, until he passed out.  
**NARRATOR:** He never came back up.  

## Scene 6: Don Shirley

**Visual:** Above, a second light (Shirley) sees the still light below and descends. At -250 m a crack/warning on his rebreather unit; he turns back. A slate. Then a very long climb up the decompression staircase, the world tilting (vertigo).

**NARRATOR:** His friend Don Shirley saw Dave's light below, not moving, and went down after him.  
**NARRATOR:** At two hundred and fifty metres, the pressure crushed his rebreather's controls. He had to turn back.  
**NARRATOR:** It took him twelve and a half hours to get back to the surface.  

## Scene 7: Aftermath

**Visual:** Four days later: the line is lifted; two soft lights rise together through the water toward the surface caustics. The pool, the plaque. End on the line 'Dave Shaw did bring Deon home.' and loop.

**NARRATOR:** Four days later, as divers lifted the line, Dave and Deon rose to the surface together.  
**NARRATOR:** Dave Shaw did bring Deon home.
